# Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)


## 1. Net Energy Expense
**Formula:**  
Net Energy Expense = Sum(Energy Expense) - Sum(Energy Revenue)

**Description:**  
The Net Energy Expense measures the total cost of energy consumed by the system after accounting for revenue generated from energy exports.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Lower is better**: A lower value indicates that the system is either consuming less energy or generating more revenue from exports, resulting in cost savings.

---

## 2. Tip Potential
**Formula:**  
Tip Potential = Sum(Tip Potential Values)

**Description:**  
This metric represents the total potential for improvement or optimization in energy storage and distribution, based on ideal trading and operation.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Higher is better**: A higher value suggests that the system has significant room for optimization, which could be capitalized on through better decision-making or infrastructure improvements.

---

## 3. Tip Actual
**Formula:**  
Tip Actual = Sum(Tip Actual Values)

**Description:**  
Tip Actual reflects how much of the optimization potential is actually realized in the current setup.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Higher is better**: A higher value means that the system is operating closer to its theoretical optimization potential.

---

## 4. Self-Consumption
**Formula:**  
Self-Consumption = (Sum(Self-Consumption) / Sum(Exported Energy)) * 100

**Description:**  
Self-consumption refers to the percentage of locally generated energy that is consumed internally rather than exported to the grid.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Higher is better**: A higher self-consumption percentage indicates greater energy independence, as more locally generated energy is used within the system.

---

## 5. Energy Demand
**Formula:**  
Energy Demand = Sum(Imported Energy)

**Description:**  
Energy Demand represents the total amount of energy imported from external sources.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Lower is better**: Lower energy demand indicates that the system is more self-sufficient and relies less on external energy sources.

---

## 6. Gross Self-Consumption Rate
**Formula:**  
Gross Self-Consumption Rate = (Sum(Self-Consumption) / (Sum(Imported Energy) + Sum(Self-Consumption))) * 100

**Description:**  
This KPI measures the proportion of self-consumed energy relative to the total energy demand (including imported energy and self-consumption).

**Interpretation:**  
- **Higher is better**: A higher gross self-consumption rate means that a larger share of the system's energy demand is met by self-generated energy.

---

## 7. Net Metering
**Formula:**  
Net Metering = Sum(Self-Consumption) - Sum(Exported Energy)

**Description:**  
Net Metering calculates the difference between the amount of energy consumed internally and the energy exported to the grid.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Closer to zero is better**: A balance between self-consumption and exported energy is often ideal for maximizing efficiency.

---

## 8. Cost per Unit Energy
**Formula:**  
Cost per Unit Energy = Net Energy Expense / Energy Demand

**Description:**  
This KPI shows the cost incurred for each unit of energy imported or consumed by the system.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Lower is better**: A lower cost per unit energy means the system is more cost-efficient in meeting its energy demands.

---

## 9. Revenue per Unit Energy
**Formula:**  
Revenue per Unit Energy = Sum(Energy Revenue) / Sum(Exported Energy)

**Description:**  
This KPI calculates how much revenue is generated for each unit of energy exported to the grid.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Higher is better**: A higher revenue per unit exported energy indicates that the system is able to generate significant income from excess energy.

---

## 10. Energy Balance
**Formula:**  
Energy Balance = Sum(Imported Energy) - (Sum(Self-Consumption) + Sum(Exported Energy))

**Description:**  
Energy Balance measures the overall energy flow, indicating how much energy the system is importing versus what it is using or exporting.

**Interpretation:**  
- **Closer to zero is better**: A balanced system (close to zero) means that it is meeting its energy needs efficiently without relying heavily on imports or exports.

---

# KPI Comparisons and Interpretation

When comparing different systems or scenarios based on these KPIs, the interpretation typically follows these guidelines:

- **Net Energy Expense**: Lower values indicate better performance as the system is minimizing energy costs.
- **Tip Potential & Actual**: Higher values are favorable as they show either the potential or the actual realization of energy system optimizations.
- **Self-Consumption & Gross Self-Consumption Rate**: Higher values indicate greater energy independence and reduced reliance on external sources.
- **Energy Demand**: Lower values are better as they indicate less reliance on external energy imports.
- **Net Metering**: Values closer to zero are ideal, showing a balanced use of self-generated energy and exports.
- **Cost per Unit Energy**: Lower values are preferable as they indicate cost efficiency in meeting energy demand.
- **Revenue per Unit Energy**: Higher values are better, suggesting that the system generates more revenue from exported energy.
- **Energy Balance**: A balanced system (near zero) is often ideal for efficiency and self-sufficiency.

For each KPI, the "better" value may vary depending on the specific objectives of the system being analyzed, such as cost savings, energy independence, or optimization potential.
